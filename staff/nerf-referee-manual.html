Nerf Referee Training Manual

Field guide for a two-hour hosted game session. Updated November 13, 2026.

Your job: Set up a safe battlefield, explain and enforce the rules, keep every player included, and leave the host ready for the rest of the overnight rental.

Before you accept an assignment

  • You are at least 16 years old, can drive, and have lawful, reliable transportation to and from the assignment. Follow all license restrictions.
  • You have signed The Party Porch referee waiver before working. Ask the owner for the waiver; this manual is not a waiver.
  • You know the event address, party start time, player count, assigned crew, equipment list and owner contact.
  • The owner has confirmed the required customer/participant waiver paperwork is complete. A booking, invoice or payment is not a signed waiver; report missing paperwork before play.
  • The host has confirmed a hose with running water and an accessible electrical outlet for setup.
  • You have reviewed the customer guide and practiced setup, blaster operation and the games before working independently.

Professional expectations

  • Wear practical closed-toe shoes and clothing suitable for active outdoor work. Bring drinking water and a charged phone.
  • Be calm, fair and encouraging. No rough play, inappropriate language, public shaming or unauthorized customer photos.
  • An adult host must remain available. A referee, especially one under 18, is not a replacement for adult supervision.
  • Do not promise discounts, refunds, extra time or changes to the booking. Contact the owner for approval.

The Party Porch / Setup & safety / 2 of 5

Build the field. Check every item.

Do not start if the play area is unsafe, water or electricity is unavailable, an obstacle is unstable, or players cannot wear suitable eye protection. Pause and contact the owner rather than improvising.

Setup sequence

  1. Walk the site with the host. Use flat, open ground away from traffic, pools, grills, sharp objects, breakables and other hazards. Remove debris. Confirm safe access to toilets and drinking water.
  2. Confirm utilities. The host provides the hose, running water and outlet. Keep electrical connections and the pump dry and away from filling/draining water. Follow equipment instructions; do not use damaged equipment or unsafe power connections.
  3. Lay out obstacles. Create balanced cover on both sides, clear sightlines and enough room to move without collisions. Never block exits or walkways.
  4. Add water first. Put one full gallon of water in every inflatable. Water and air use the same shared, stacked dual-valve assembly, not separate fill points elsewhere on the obstacle.
  5. Inflate through that same assembly. Use the supplied pump until the obstacle is upright and firm; do not overinflate. Close the valve securely and check for leaks and instability. Do not substitute different anchoring instructions.
  6. Mark the field. Identify boundaries, two bases, respawn areas, a gear station and a safe spectator/rest area outside the line of fire. Put the hose away so it is not a trip hazard.
  7. Inspect gear. Count blasters, approved darts and glasses against the supplied inventory. Remove damaged items. Test blasters safely away from people. Never look down a barrel or modify equipment.

Player briefing: say this before the first round

“Glasses stay on while a game is active. Aim below shoulders, never at faces. Stay inside these boundaries. No tackling, pushing, grabbing blasters or climbing on obstacles. Keep space between players; no point-blank shots.”

“When you hear PAUSE, stop moving and firing, lower your blaster and listen. Anyone can call a pause for a safety issue. When hit, raise your hand or blaster, stop firing and follow this round's respawn rule.”

“If your glasses fall off or your blaster jams, stop and call me. Keep your glasses on until everyone has stopped firing. Spectators stay in the safe area.”

Ready-to-play check

  • Every player has well-fitting eye protection; spectators are clear of the field.
  • Every player can repeat the pause signal, boundaries and hit/respawn rules.
  • Teams are balanced for age, size and experience; younger players get a simple demonstration.
  • The host is present, obstacles are stable and nobody is handling the hose or electrical setup in the play area.
Recheck the field between rounds. Stop immediately for unsafe weather, equipment movement, lost glasses or an injured player.
The Party Porch / Session plan & games / 3 of 5

Run a fun, organized two hours.

Use this as a sample 120-minute hosted session, including breaks. Adjust games to player needs and conditions, not by skipping safety or extending the paid session without approval.

0–15 minWelcome, glasses, boundaries, safety briefing, team split and practice round.
15–40 minShort Team Elimination rounds; rotate teams and explain hit calls.
40–50 minWater/rest break, dart collection with all firing stopped, field check.
50–80 minCapture the Flag and Protect the MVP; switch sides between rounds.
80–90 minWater/rest break and equipment check. Add breaks sooner whenever needed.
90–115 minChoose a favorite game or King of the Fort; finish with balanced teams.
115–120 minFinal cease-fire, count gear, thank players and transition to the host handoff.

Six games to learn

Team Elimination / 3–5 min

Two teams start at opposite bases. A hit player raises a hand, stops firing and leaves by the safe route. No respawns. Last team with a player wins. Keep rounds short so nobody sits out for long.

Capture the Flag / 8–12 min

Put a flag at each base. Hit players return to base, count to 10 and rejoin. No guarding within 10 feet of a flag. First team to bring the opposing flag back to its own base wins. No grabbing or tugging flags from players.

Protect the MVP / 6–10 min

Each team chooses one MVP with three lives. After each hit, the MVP raises a hand and reports a lost life. Others respawn at base after a 10-count. First team to remove the opposing MVP's three lives wins.

Medic / 8 min

Choose one medic per team. Hit players raise a hand and stop firing; keep them clear of active running paths. The medic safely tags a shoulder to revive them. Hit medics respawn at base after a 15-count. Count opposing hits; most points wins. Pause if a player is exposed to collisions.

Zombie Outbreak / 5 min

One or two zombies gently hand-tag survivors; a tagged survivor becomes a zombie. One dart hit freezes a zombie for a 10-count. Survivors win if anyone remains at time. Use walking-only tags, no grabbing or chasing into hazards; skip this game if contact cannot be kept safe.

King of the Fort / 5 min per side

Mark a scoring zone beside an obstacle, never on top of it. Attackers score by holding the zone for five seconds. Hit players return to base for a 10-count. Attackers try to score three times, then teams swap roles. No climbing or body-blocking.

Before each round: explain the objective, hit rule, respawn rule, time limit and win condition. End with a clear cease-fire before collecting darts.
The Party Porch / Field leadership & incidents / 4 of 5

Stay fair. Stop problems early.

Younger or mixed-age groups

Use short rounds, simple objectives and frequent respawns. Demonstrate rather than lecture. Balance teams by size and confidence; do not let older players dominate or target one child repeatedly.

Positioning & multiple referees

Keep the whole field in view; move to cover blind spots without standing in firing lanes. Agree who calls starts/stops, watches each side and handles the host. If you cannot supervise safely, pause and contact the owner.

Disputed hits or scores

Keep decisions brief and neutral: “We will reset and keep playing.” Never encourage players to prove a hit by shooting again. Repeated arguments mean a pause and host involvement.

Unsafe behavior

Stop the immediate action, explain the rule calmly and involve the adult host. A player who will not follow safety rules must leave active play until safe participation is agreed. Do not physically discipline or restrain players.

If someone is hurt

  1. Call PAUSE. Stop all firing, clear space and keep other players away. Bring the adult host immediately.
  2. Call 911 for a serious injury or medical emergency. Give the event address and follow dispatcher instructions. Notify the owner after emergency help is underway.
  3. Give only assistance within your training. Do not move an injured person unless there is immediate danger or emergency personnel direct you. Do not give medications or diagnose an injury.
  4. For an eye injury, stop play and have the host obtain prompt medical guidance. Do not let the player simply put glasses back on and continue.
  5. Document the time, location, what you observed, who was notified and what action was taken. Keep customer information private; share it only with the host, owner and responders as appropriate.

Weather, heat & equipment problems

  • Thunder, lightning or unsafe wind: Stop outdoor play, tell the host and move to appropriate shelter. Do not use inflatables as shelter. Contact the owner about cancellation or restarting safely.
  • Heat, fatigue or distress: Pause for water and shade/cooling; add breaks as needed. Seek emergency help if someone is seriously unwell. Never pressure a child to continue.
  • Unstable obstacle, leak, damaged glasses or jammed blaster: Remove the item from play. Stop the round if the field is unsafe; do not improvise repairs around active players.
Restart only after the hazard is resolved. Safety takes priority over the clock, scores or customer pressure. Do not promise compensation; refer booking decisions to the owner.

The Party Porch / Closeout & training signoff / 5 of 5

Finish well. Leave a clear handoff.

The standard Nerf rental stays overnight. The end of the referee's two-hour session does not automatically end the rental or mean the equipment is collected. Follow the booking's agreed pickup date and time.

Before leaving the host

  • Call a final cease-fire. Collect stray darts, count equipment against the inventory and report missing or damaged items to the owner.
  • Explain that referee supervision is ending. The adult host is responsible for supervising any continued play and enforcing glasses, boundaries and safe-use rules.
  • Show the host the shared dual-valve assembly and the deflation/draining process. Do not leave them guessing which valve to use.
  • Confirm the agreed pickup date and time from the booking. If it is missing or unclear, contact the owner; do not invent a pickup window.
  • Share the customer guide and explain the bin/porch return requirements below.
  • Report completion, equipment issues, incidents and any unresolved arrangements to the owner. Never collect or change a payment unless explicitly authorized.

Return instructions to explain to the host

Everything goes back in the bin. All blasters, darts, safety glasses, inflatables and the pump must be returned. Place the packed bin on the porch for the agreed pickup date and time.
  1. Wipe off dirt, grass, mud, food and spills.
  2. Open the shared valve assembly and fully deflate every inflatable.
  3. Drain all water through that same assembly, tilting in different directions until no water remains.
  4. Wipe every item dry and let damp surfaces air-dry completely. Do not pack wet or damp equipment.
  5. Close the valves only after the inflatables are empty and dry. Pack all equipment into the bin and put it on the porch for pickup.

Trainer signoff before independent assignments

  • Eligibility and signed referee waiver verified.
  • Demonstrated water-first setup, pump use and correct shared-valve operation.
  • Delivered the safety briefing and demonstrated a clear pause/cease-fire.
  • Explained and ran at least two games; handled a mock hit dispute fairly.
  • Explained emergency escalation, owner reporting and the overnight host handoff.
  • Demonstrated dry, empty bin packing and explained the agreed porch pickup.
